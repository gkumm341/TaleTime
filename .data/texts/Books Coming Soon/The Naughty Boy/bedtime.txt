The Naughty Boy

(A calm bedtime retelling)


On a bright morning, when the air smelled of summer, a poet sat by an open window.

He was writing.

Or trying to.

Because his thoughts kept wandering.


Suddenly, there was a tap at the glass.

The poet looked up.

And there, on the window ledge, sat a small boy.


He was not dressed like an ordinary child.

He had a bow.

He had arrows.

And his eyes sparkled with mischief.


The poet sighed.

“I know who you are,” he said.

“You are Cupid.

The naughty boy.”


Cupid grinned.

He swung his feet.

And before the poet could say another word…

Cupid raised his bow.


“Don’t!” the poet cried.


But the arrow flew.

Not into the air.

Not into the wall.


Straight into the poet’s heart.


The poet gasped.

Not because it hurt terribly—

but because it made his thoughts tumble.


Suddenly he remembered faces.

Smiles.

Kind voices.

He felt warmth.

He felt longing.

He felt as if his heart had woken up.


Cupid laughed softly.

“That is what I do,” he said.


The poet rubbed his chest.

He tried to look stern.

But it was hard.

Because even as he complained, he felt more alive.


“Why are you so naughty?” the poet asked.


Cupid tilted his head.

“Because love is powerful,” he said.

“And people forget.

They think they can control everything.

But love arrives when it wants.”


With that, Cupid hopped off the ledge.

He vanished like a sunbeam.


The poet sat quietly.

His page was still blank.

But his heart was full.


He began to write.

Not just clever lines.

But honest ones.


And he understood a gentle lesson:

That love can surprise us.

That love can soften us.

And that even a “naughty boy” can bring something good.


The End!
