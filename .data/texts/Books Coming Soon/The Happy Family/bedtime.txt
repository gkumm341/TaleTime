The Happy Family

(A calm bedtime retelling)


On a sunny day in the garden, a leaf lay open like a tiny stage.

And on that leaf lived a happy family.


They were not a family of people.

They were a family of snails.


There was the mother snail.

The father snail.

And their little snail children, as round and shiny as dewdrops.


The snail family did not rush.

They did not worry about clocks.

They did not chase noisy things.


They had one great dream.

To see the world.


But their world was small.

A patch of green.

A stem.

A stone warmed by the sun.

And beyond that… a garden full of mysteries.


Each day, the snail parents taught their children something gentle.

“Always carry your home with you,” the mother snail said.

“Not only your shell,” the father snail added,

“But your calmness.”


The snail children listened.

They practiced stretching their little feelers.

They learned to taste the air.

They learned to wait when the wind was too strong.


One morning, the snail family decided it was time.

They would travel… all the way to the biggest plant they could see.

A tall stalk with broad leaves.


It looked like a mountain to them.


They began their journey.

Slowly.

Steadily.

Together.


Along the way, they met a beetle.

He buzzed proudly.

He said he was busy.

He said he was important.


The snails smiled politely.

They did not envy him.

Because their happiness did not depend on being admired.


They met an ant.

He marched and marched.

He spoke of work.

He spoke of hurry.


The snails nodded.

And continued at their own pace.


At last, they reached the tall plant.

The leaf was wide.

The view was bright.

They could see more of the garden than ever before.


The snail children gasped.

Not loudly.

But with wonder.


“Is this the whole world?” one asked.


The mother snail laughed softly.

“Oh no,” she said.

“The world is always bigger.

But we don’t need to see it all at once.”


They sat together.

They felt the warmth of the sun.

They listened to the birds.

They watched a cloud drift like a slow ship across the sky.


And the snail family was happy.

Not because they were fast.

Not because they were grand.

But because they were together.


The End!
