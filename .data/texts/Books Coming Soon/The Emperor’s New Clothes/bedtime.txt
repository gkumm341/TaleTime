The Emperor’s New Clothes

(A calm bedtime retelling)


Long ago, in a bright and busy city, there lived an Emperor who loved beautiful clothes.

Not brave deeds.

Not wise books.

But velvet. Silk. Satin.

He changed his outfit in the morning… and again at noon… and again before supper.

And the people would whisper, smiling, “There goes the Emperor… wearing something new again.”


One day, two strangers arrived.

They bowed low.

“We are the finest weavers in the world,” they said.

“We can make cloth so wonderful… so rare… that it cannot be seen by anyone who is foolish… or unfit for their job.”

The Emperor’s eyes grew wide.

“A suit like that,” he thought, “would tell me who is clever… and who is not.”

And he loved that idea even more than he loved his coats.


He gave the strangers gold.

He gave them a grand room.

He gave them silk… and the finest thread.

“Begin at once,” he said.


The two strangers set up empty looms.

They moved their hands as if they were weaving.

They snipped the air with scissors.

They measured nothing with a tape.

But they asked for more and more gold… and more and more silk…

And they hid it away.


After a while, the Emperor grew curious.

But he felt a tiny worry.

“What if… I cannot see the cloth?”

He did not want anyone to think he was foolish.

So he called his oldest minister.

“Go,” he said, “and see how the weaving is going.”


The minister walked into the room.

He looked at the looms.

He saw… nothing.

Not a single thread.

His stomach turned cold.

“Surely the cloth is there,” he told himself.

“I must not be foolish. I must not be unfit.”

So he smiled and said, “Oh! It is splendid! So light! So delicate!”


The strangers nodded.

“See how fine the pattern is,” they said.

“See how lovely the colors are.”

And the minister nodded again.


He hurried back to the Emperor.

“It is marvelous,” he said.

“The most beautiful cloth I have ever seen.”


The Emperor sent another important person.

Then another.

Each one looked.

Each one saw nothing.

And each one praised the cloth.

Because each one feared the same thing.


At last, the Emperor went himself.

He walked with many people.

The doors opened.

The looms stood there.

Empty.


The Emperor’s heart thumped.

He stared and stared.

He saw… nothing.


But he lifted his chin.

“How magnificent,” he said.

And everyone behind him nodded.

“Yes. Magnificent,” they agreed.


The strangers said, “Now we shall dress you.”

They held up invisible trousers.

They buttoned an invisible coat.

They placed an invisible cape on his shoulders.

They fussed and flattered.

And the Emperor stood very still.


“Look at the train!” one cried.

“It flows like water!”


And the Emperor turned slowly before the mirror.

He smiled as if he could see the finest clothes in the world.


Soon it was time for the grand parade.

The Emperor walked beneath bright banners.

His servants pretended to carry the long, invisible robe.

The crowd cheered.

Because everyone feared being called foolish.


But then…

A small child looked up.

A child who did not worry about being important.

A child who simply said what was true.


“He isn’t wearing anything,” the child said.


The words floated through the air.

Soft at first.

Then stronger.


“He isn’t wearing anything,” someone whispered.

“And he truly isn’t,” someone else said.


The Emperor felt the truth like a cold wind.

He wanted to run.

He wanted to hide.

But the parade was already moving.

So he held his head high… and kept walking.


And in that moment, the people learned something gentle and important.

That it is safer to speak the truth…

Than to pretend.


That night, the Emperor sat quietly.

He thought about the child.

He thought about honesty.

And he decided that tomorrow…

He would try to be wise.

Not only well-dressed.
