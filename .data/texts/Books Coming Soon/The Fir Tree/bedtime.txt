The Fir Tree

(A calm bedtime retelling)


In a quiet forest, where the air smelled of pine and snow, a little fir tree grew.

He was green.

He was straight.

And he was always looking up.


Above him were tall trees with wide branches.

Birds sang in their tops.

Sunlight spilled in warm patches on the moss.


But the little fir tree did not feel happy.

He did not want to be small.

He did not want to wait.


“I wish I were taller,” he thought.

“I wish I were grown already.”


The wind whispered through his needles.

The older trees seemed to say,

“Be patient.

Be here.

Enjoy the day you have.”


But the fir tree kept dreaming of tomorrow.

He dreamed of being carried away.

He dreamed of bright lights.

He dreamed of something grand.


Seasons passed.

Snow fell softly.

Spring returned.

Summer warmed the forest floor.

And the fir tree grew.


One winter, men came into the forest.

They chose tall trees.

They cut them down.

And carried them away.


The fir tree trembled with excitement.

“Oh! How lucky they are,” he thought.

“They are going to something important.”


Soon, the men came again.

This time, they stopped near the fir tree.

They measured him.

They nodded.


And with a quick chop…

His roots were no longer in the earth.


The fir tree felt a sudden emptiness.

The forest smell faded.

The moss was gone.

The birds were gone.


He was carried away.


At first, he rode on a cart.

He saw roads.

Houses.

People.


Then he was brought inside a warm room.

Candles waited.

Ornaments shimmered.

Children’s eyes sparkled.


They stood him upright.

They dressed him in ribbons.

They hung golden apples.

They lit candles that flickered like tiny stars.


The fir tree was amazed.

“It is even more beautiful than I dreamed,” he thought.


And for one night…

He truly was the center of everything.

There was music.

Laughter.

Sweet cakes.

Soft voices singing.


But when morning came, the candles were out.

The children ran away.

The room grew quiet.


The fir tree waited for more joy.

For more songs.

For another night of light.


But the people carried him away again.

Up a narrow staircase.

Into a dusty attic.


There were no candles.

No ribbons.

No children.

Only silence.


The fir tree began to remember the forest.

The cool snow.

The gentle wind.

The smell of earth.


He thought of the sun warming his needles.

He thought of being rooted.

He thought of being alive in the simplest way.


And he felt a soft regret.

“I was so busy wanting the next thing,” he whispered,

“I forgot to love what I already had.”


Time passed.

One day, the fir tree was taken down.

He was chopped into pieces.

And used to warm a fire.


The flames crackled.

The room glowed.

And for a moment, the fir tree felt warmth again.

Not as bright as candles.

Not as loud as laughter.

But gentle.


And as the last sparks drifted upward, the fir tree’s final thought was quiet and true:

That the best days are often the ones we live right now.


The End!
