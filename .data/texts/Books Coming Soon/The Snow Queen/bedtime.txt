The Snow Queen

(A calm bedtime retelling)


In a town where roses grew in window boxes, two children lived side by side.

Their names were Gerda and Kay.

They were friends like sunshine and spring.


In summer, they played among the flowers.

In winter, they sat by the stove and listened to stories.


One night, someone told a tale about the Snow Queen.

“She rides in the cold wind,” the story said.

“Her palace is made of ice.

And her kiss can make a heart feel numb.”


Kay laughed.

Gerda shivered.

But the roses in the window looked brave.


Not long after, a strange thing happened.

Kay rubbed his eye.

He frowned.


A tiny splinter—sharp and cold—had found its way in.

And another splinter slipped into his heart.


From that day, Kay changed.

He began to speak sharply.

He mocked the roses.

He teased Gerda.


Gerda did not understand.

But she kept loving him.

Because love, when it is gentle, does not give up easily.


Then winter came.

Snow covered the streets.

The river froze.

And the air sparkled like glass.


One evening, Kay went out with his little sled.

The snow swirled.

A white carriage appeared.

And in it sat a tall lady, wrapped in pale fur.

Her eyes were like winter stars.


She smiled.

She leaned down.

And she kissed Kay on the forehead.


At once, he stopped trembling from the cold.

He felt strong.

He felt fearless.

He felt… far away.


And the Snow Queen carried him off.


Gerda cried.

Days passed.

People said Kay was gone.

But Gerda did not believe her friend had vanished forever.


So she set out.

Small feet.

Big courage.

A heart full of warm thoughts.


She walked beyond the town.

She asked the river.

She asked the birds.

She asked the trees.


Some could not help.

Some did not know.

But Gerda kept going.


She met a kind woman who gave her bread.

She met a girl who shared her mittens.

She met a reindeer who knew the northern winds.


And all along the way, Gerda’s love stayed steady.

Like a candle that does not go out.


At last, she reached the Snow Queen’s palace.

It was a place of glittering ice.

The walls shone.

The floors were smooth.

Everything was beautiful… and silent.


In the great hall, Kay sat alone.

He was trying to fit pieces of ice together.

He stared at them with cold eyes.


He did not smile.

He did not look up.


Gerda ran to him.

“Kay!” she cried.


At first, he did not recognize her.

The splinter in his heart held him tight.


But Gerda leaned close.

And her tears fell—warm and real—onto his chest.


The warmth melted the icy splinter.

It slipped away.


Then Kay blinked.

The splinter in his eye loosened.

And suddenly he saw Gerda as she truly was.


His face softened.

He began to cry.

And with those tears, the last cold piece slid out.


Gerda and Kay held each other.

The palace no longer felt so powerful.

Because love was stronger than ice.


They walked away.

Through snow and wind.

Past quiet fields.

Back toward roses and warm windows.


And when they returned home, they were older.

Not in years.

But in understanding.


They knew that winter can be frightening.

But spring returns.

And a loving heart can guide the way.


The End!
