The Little Match Girl

(A calm bedtime retelling)


On a cold winter night, snow drifted softly through the streets.

Windows glowed with warm light.

The air smelled of smoke and supper.


And in the shadow of the tall houses, a little girl walked alone.

She carried a bundle of matches.

Her shoes were thin.

Her hands were red from the cold.


“Matches,” she whispered.

“Won’t you buy a match?”


But people hurried past.

They had warm homes to reach.

They had hands tucked into pockets.

They did not look down.


The little girl’s stomach growled.

The night felt very wide.

And she did not want to go home empty-handed.


At last, she found a corner between two houses.

The wind was softer there.

She crouched down.

She hugged her knees.


She was so cold.

So she struck one match.


A tiny flame appeared.

Small.

Brave.

Warm as a gentle whisper.


In its light, she imagined a stove.

A red, glowing stove.

A place to warm her hands.

A place to breathe without shivering.


Then the match went out.

And the stove was gone.


So she struck another.

This time she imagined a table.

A table set for supper.

Steam rising from a bowl.

Bread.

Soup.

A peaceful room.


Then the match went out.

And the table faded.


She struck a third match.

Now she saw a Christmas tree.

Tall and shining.

Candles like little stars.

Ornaments like tiny moons.


The tree seemed to sparkle brighter and brighter…

Until it drifted upward into the night.


The little girl’s eyes filled with tears.

Not only from cold.

But from longing.


She struck one more match.

And in its warm light, she saw a kind face.

Someone she had loved.

Someone gentle.

Someone safe.


The little girl smiled.

And she held the match close.

Because she did not want the warm feeling to disappear.


So she lit another.

And another.

Until the little corner was filled with soft light.


And in that glow, the night felt less frightening.

The snow felt quieter.

And the little girl felt held.


When morning came, the street was still.

But the winter sky looked pale and calm.


And though the world was cold,

the story reminds us to be kind.

To notice.

To help.

To warm one another when we can.


So tonight, as you rest,

let your heart be gentle.

And let your dreams be warm.


The End!
