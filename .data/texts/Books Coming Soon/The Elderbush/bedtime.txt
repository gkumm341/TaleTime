The Elderbush

(A calm bedtime retelling)


In a small room under a sloping roof, a little boy lay in bed.

He had been ill.

Not terribly.

But enough that his cheeks were warm and his eyes felt heavy.


Outside, the night was quiet.

A soft wind brushed the window.

And the streetlamp made a pale circle of light on the ground.


The boy’s mother sat beside him.

She smoothed his hair.

She spoke in a gentle voice.


“Rest,” she said.

“Tomorrow will come.”


But the boy could not sleep.

He listened to the ticking clock.

He watched the shadows.

And he felt lonely in the dark.


Then the old neighbor came in.

He was kind.

He smelled faintly of herbs and warm tea.


He carried a small pot.

In it grew an elderbush.

Just a little plant.

Green and patient.


“This,” the neighbor said, “is a good plant for comfort.

Its flowers can make a sweet tea.

And its leaves have stories.”


The boy blinked.

“Stories?”


The neighbor smiled.

He poured a warm drink.

And soon the room felt softer.


As the boy sipped, his eyes began to droop.

And then…

Something strange and lovely happened.


He imagined the elderbush growing taller.

Branches stretching.

Leaves whispering.


And in the leaves, he saw pictures.

Not sharp pictures, like a painting.

But dreamy ones.

Like memories.


He saw summertime.

A garden.

A bench beneath a tree.

He saw his grandmother’s hands.

He saw her smile.


He heard her voice, gentle as a lullaby.

“Life is made of seasons,” it seemed to say.

“Some are bright.

Some are cold.

But all of them pass.

And love remains.”


The boy’s breathing slowed.

The worries in his chest felt lighter.


The elderbush in the pot did not truly grow into a forest.

But the boy’s heart grew calmer.

And that was the real magic.


In the morning, he woke with clearer eyes.

The neighbor had gone.

The room was bright.

And the little elderbush sat quietly on the sill.


The boy touched a leaf.

He remembered the dream.

And he smiled.


Because sometimes, when we are tired,

a small kindness…

and a gentle story…

are enough to help us heal.


The End!
