The Leap-Frog

(A calm bedtime retelling)


In a green ditch near a garden path, a leap-frog sat on a lily leaf.

He was clever.

He was quick.

And he thought very highly of himself.


He could jump far.

He could sing at night.

And he believed that made him important.


One day, something surprising arrived.

A little lady-frog came hopping nearby.

She was graceful.

She was calm.

And she had kind eyes.


The leap-frog puffed himself up.

“I am the finest frog here,” he said.

“I will marry her.

Of course I will.”


But the lady-frog simply listened.

She did not hurry.

She did not flatter.


Soon, visitors came to the ditch.

Two old snails, slow and quiet.

They were the lady-frog’s aunts.


They wore moss on their shells like little shawls.

And they blinked slowly.


“We have heard much about you,” they said to the leap-frog.

“Tell us… what makes you a good match?”


The leap-frog boasted.

He spoke of his leaps.

He spoke of his loud songs.

He spoke of how everyone noticed him.


The snails listened.

And then one of them asked gently,

“But will you be kind?

Will you be steady?

Will you care for her when the sun is gone… and the pond is dark?”


The leap-frog did not like those questions.

They sounded ordinary.

They sounded slow.


He wanted to be admired.

Not measured.


So he tried to impress them.

He jumped.

And he jumped again.

And he jumped so high he almost tumbled over.


The snails nodded politely.

“Very lively,” they said.


But the lady-frog did not look amazed.

She looked thoughtful.


Days passed.

The leap-frog waited for her to choose him.

But she did not.


Instead, she listened to the garden.

She watched the water.

She spoke kindly to those who were kind.


And when she finally hopped away to begin her life, she went quietly.

Without loud announcements.

Without bragging.


The leap-frog felt a sting of pride.

He told himself she must have been wrong.

But the ditch grew still.


That night, as the moon reflected on the water, the leap-frog thought.

He remembered the snails’ questions.

He remembered the lady-frog’s calm eyes.


And for the first time, he wondered if being important was not the same as being good.


He did not become perfect overnight.

But he began to listen more.

He began to boast less.

He began to notice others.


And that is a kind of growing, too.

Quiet.

Slow.

But real.


The End!
