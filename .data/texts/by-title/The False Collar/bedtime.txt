The False Collar

(A calm bedtime retelling)


In a tidy drawer, among ribbons and buttons, lived a stiff white collar.

It was not part of a person.

It was only a collar.

But it had a very large opinion of itself.


“I am so fine,” the collar thought.

“So bright.

So proper.

So important.”


It had been worn once.

Only once.


And now it lay waiting.

Waiting to be admired again.


One day, the collar was taken out.

It was brushed.

It was smoothed.

It was made perfectly crisp.


The collar felt proud.

“At last,” it thought, “I will be seen.”


As it lay on the table, it noticed other things nearby.

A soft stocking.

A quiet garter.

A little lace ribbon.


The collar puffed itself up.

It tried to impress them.

It spoke as if it were very grand.


“I belong to someone important,” it said.

“Someone who must look excellent.

Someone who needs me.”


The stocking did not answer.

The ribbon did not brag.

They were simply… useful.


The collar did not like that.

So it began to tell stories about itself.

Big stories.

Shiny stories.


Then something happened.

When it was washed, the collar cracked.

Just a little.

But enough.


And suddenly, it was not perfect anymore.


The collar felt shocked.

“How can I be admired now?” it wondered.


Time passed.

The collar was used less.

And then not at all.


At last, it was tossed aside.

Not with cruelty.

Just… because it was no longer needed.


And in the quiet, the collar began to understand.

Being stiff and showy was not the same as being valuable.


The ribbon, the stocking, the simple things—

They were worn.

They were washed.

They were loved.


Because they helped.


So the collar, with its bright dreams and tiny crack, learned a gentle lesson.

That the best kind of pride is quiet.

And the best kind of beauty is usefulness.
