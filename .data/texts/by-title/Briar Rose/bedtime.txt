Briar Rose

A calm bedtime retelling


Long ago, in a peaceful kingdom far away, there lived a King and a Queen who seemed to have everything anyone could wish for.

They had fine clothes.
They had warm homes.
They had kind people around them.

But there was one thing they did not have.

A child.

And this made their hearts very heavy.

One gentle morning, as the Queen walked beside the quiet river at the bottom of the garden, she saw a little fish lying on the bank, gasping for air.

The Queen felt sorry for it.

She lifted the tiny creature carefully and placed it back into the water.

Before swimming away, the fish lifted its head and said softly,

“I know your wish.
And for your kindness… it shall come true.
You will soon have a daughter.”

And so it happened.

Before long, the Queen gave birth to a beautiful baby girl.

The King was filled with joy and ordered a great feast so the whole kingdom could celebrate.

He invited his family.
His friends.
His nobles.

And the Queen said,

“Let us invite the fairies as well, so they may bless our child.”

There were thirteen fairies in the kingdom.

But the King and Queen owned only twelve golden dishes.

And so, without meaning to be unkind, they invited only twelve fairies to the feast.

The twelve fairies came dressed in shining clothes.

Each carried a wand.

And one by one, they gave their gifts to the baby princess.

One gave her kindness.
One gave her beauty.
One gave her wisdom.
One gave her gentleness.
And so on…

Until the child seemed blessed with every good thing in the world.


Just as the last fairy was about to finish…

A loud noise echoed through the palace.

The doors opened.

And in came the thirteenth fairy.

She wore dark clothes.
She carried a broomstick.
And her eyes were full of anger.

“You did not invite me,” she said.
“And now I will give my own gift.”

She raised her voice and cried,

“When the princess is fifteen years old, she shall prick her finger on a spindle…
and fall down dead.”

The room fell silent.

The Queen wept.

The King stood frozen.

But then…

The twelfth fairy stepped forward.

She could not take away the curse…
but she could soften it.

“She shall not die,” said the kind fairy.
“She shall only fall into a deep sleep for one hundred years.”

The King ordered at once that every spindle in the kingdom be burned.

He hoped to protect his daughter from all harm.

And as the years passed, the princess grew into a gentle and lovely young girl.

Everyone who met her loved her.


On the very day she turned fifteen, the King and Queen were away from the palace.

The princess wandered through the quiet halls.

Up a narrow staircase…
to a tiny door…
that she had never seen before.

Inside sat an old woman spinning quietly.

“What are you doing?” asked the princess kindly.

“Spinning,” said the woman, humming as the wheel turned.

The princess watched with wonder.

She reached out…

And touched the spindle.

At once, the old spell awakened.

The spindle pricked her finger.

And the princess fell gently to the floor…

Into a deep, peaceful sleep.

When the King and Queen returned, sorrow filled the palace.

But something strange happened.

As the princess slept…

Everyone else fell asleep too.

The King.
The Queen.
The servants.
The guards.
The horses in the stables.
The dogs in the yard.
Even the birds on the roof.

The fire in the hearth went still.
The cook froze with her spoon in the air.
The butler slept with his cup in hand.

And silence settled over the palace.

Soon, a great hedge of thorns grew around the castle.

Each year it grew higher.

Each year it grew thicker.

Until at last, the palace could no longer be seen.

People began to tell stories of the sleeping princess.

They called her Briar Rose.

And many brave princes came to try to reach her.

But the thorns caught them.

And none returned.


Many years passed.

Then one day, a young prince heard the story.

An old man told him of the castle hidden behind thorns.

Of the princess sleeping inside.

Of the spell that would end after one hundred years.

The prince listened quietly.

Then he said,

“I am not afraid.
I will go and see her.”

And that very day…

The hundred years came to an end.

When the prince reached the thorn hedge, it changed.

The sharp thorns became soft flowers.

They opened a path for him.

And he walked through without harm.

Inside the palace, everything was still.

The dogs slept in the yard.
The pigeons slept on the roof.
The servants slept in the halls.

He walked softly…
until he reached the highest tower.

There, in a quiet room by the window, lay Briar Rose.

She looked as peaceful as a dream.

The prince knelt beside her.

And gently…
he kissed her.


At once, Briar Rose opened her eyes.

The spell was broken.

The King awoke.
The Queen awoke.
The servants stirred.
The horses shook themselves.
The birds flew.
The fire blazed again.

Life returned to the palace.

The princess smiled at the prince.

And they walked together through the halls that had slept for a hundred years.

Soon after, they were married.

And the kingdom celebrated with joy and music.


And so Briar Rose…

Who once slept behind thorns…

Awoke to love.

And the prince who followed courage…

Found happiness.

And the kingdom learned a gentle truth.

That kindness brings blessings.
That patience brings hope.
And that even the longest night…

Can end in morning light.

THE END!
