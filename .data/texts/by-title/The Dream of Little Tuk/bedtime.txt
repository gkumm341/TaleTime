The Dream of Little Tuk

(A calm bedtime retelling)


In a small room under a roof that tapped with rain, a boy named Tuk sat with a book.

He was meant to study.

But his eyes were heavy.

His head nodded.

And the letters on the page began to blur.


Tuk tried to be brave.

He tried to sit up straight.

He tried to read one more line.


But the night was quiet.

The lamp was warm.

And soon…

Tuk fell asleep.


At first, he dreamed of the page.

Words floated like little boats.

Dates and names drifted through the air.


Then someone knocked.

Softly.

Not on the door.

But on the dream itself.


Tuk opened his eyes—inside the dream.

And he saw a gentle figure, as calm as a bedtime story.


“Come,” the figure seemed to say.

“There is a kinder way to learn.”


In the dream, Tuk was carried away.

Not roughly.

Not quickly.

But as easily as a cloud moves.


He saw places he had only read about.

A wide sea.

A bright marketplace.

A quiet countryside.


He saw people, too.

People who lived in different times.

People who worked.

People who laughed.

People who hoped.


And slowly, the dates and names began to mean something.

They were not just marks on paper.

They were stories.

Lives.

Moments.


Tuk felt wonder.

He felt curious.

He felt the world growing larger…

and friendlier.


When the dream faded, Tuk woke up in his little room.

The lamp had burned low.

The rain had softened.

The book lay open, waiting.


Tuk rubbed his eyes.

He still had studying to do.

But his heart felt lighter.


Because now he knew something:

That learning is not only about remembering.

It is about understanding.

And understanding can begin with wonder.


So Tuk read a little more.

Not with fear.

But with a quiet curiosity.


And when he finally closed the book and lay down,

his dreams returned—

gentle and bright.

THE END!
