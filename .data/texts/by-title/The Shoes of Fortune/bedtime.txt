The Shoes of Fortune.

In a city of tall roofs and quiet streets, there lived many people who wished for many things.

A poet wished for beautiful words.

A clerk wished for a better job.

A young man wished to travel.

And a woman wished her son would come home safe.


On one rainy evening, a pair of shoes arrived in a grand house.

They were not ordinary shoes.

They were made with careful hands.

And stitched with a strange kind of luck.


“Whoever wears these shoes,” someone whispered, “will have their wish come true.”


But wishes are tricky.

They can sound perfect in our minds…

And feel very different when they happen.


A servant tried on the shoes first.

“Oh,” she sighed, “I wish I could be a fine lady for just one day.”

And in a blink, she stood in a grand carriage.

People bowed.

People smiled.

People stared.


At first it felt wonderful.

But then she realized she did not know what to say.

She did not know the rules.

She did not know the names.


She missed her small kitchen.

She missed her warm tea.

She missed being herself.


So she whispered, “I wish I could go back.”

And the shoes carried her home.


Later, a young man found the shoes.

He was tired of the same streets.

The same doors.

The same weather.


“I wish I were far away,” he said.


And the shoes carried him.

Over bridges.

Over fields.

Over dark water.


He arrived somewhere new…

But it was cold.

And unfamiliar.

And lonely.


He had wished for distance.

But he had not wished for comfort.


So he wished again.

“I wish I were safely home.”

And home he went.


Another time, a poet tried the shoes.

He wished for a world where every thought could be seen.

Every dream painted on the air.


The shoes carried him into a place of bright ideas.

Clouds of colors.

Words that floated like lanterns.


It was lovely…

For a moment.


But soon it grew too loud.

Too busy.

Too full.


The poet covered his ears.

He missed the quiet.

He missed ordinary moments.

He missed a single line of honest truth.


So he wished to return.

And the shoes brought him back.


At last, the shoes were found by someone kind.

Someone who did not wish for gold.

Or fame.

Or a grand house.


This person looked at the shoes and thought quietly.

“If wishes can run away with us,” they said, “then the best wish must be a gentle one.”


So they wished for something simple.

For a warm bed.

For enough food.

For a peaceful night.

For loved ones to be safe.


And the shoes did not whisk them away.

Because those gifts were already close.

Already possible.

Already real.


The shoes were placed carefully aside.

Not as a toy.

Not as a trick.

But as a reminder.


That wishing can be sweet…

But wisdom is sweeter.


The End!
