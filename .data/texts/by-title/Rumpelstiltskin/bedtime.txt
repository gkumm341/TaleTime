Rumpelstiltskin

(A calm bedtime retelling)


Long ago, beside a quiet stream and near a whispering wood, there stood an old mill.

And in the miller’s house lived his daughter, a young woman known for her gentle ways and bright mind.

Her father loved her dearly… but he loved boasting even more.

One day, while speaking with the King, the miller said proudly,

“My daughter can spin straw into gold.”

The King’s eyes sparkled.

For the King loved gold more than anything else in the world.

That very evening, the miller’s daughter was brought to the palace.

She was led into a tall chamber filled with straw.

A spinning wheel stood in the corner.

The King spoke coldly,

“Spin this straw into gold before morning… or you shall lose your life.”

The door was locked.

The poor girl sat alone and wept.

She had no magic. No way to save herself.


Suddenly, the door opened.

In stepped a strange little man with bright eyes and a crooked smile.

“Why are you crying?” he asked kindly.

“I must spin straw into gold,” she said softly, “and I cannot.”

The little man smiled.

“I can help you… if you will give me something in return.”

She gave him her necklace.

The little man sat at the wheel and sang softly,

“Turn, turn, little wheel… Straw to gold before the dawn.”

And by morning…

The straw was gone.

And in its place…

Shone bright gold.

When the King returned, he was amazed.

But amazement turned into greed.

He led her into another room… filled with even more straw.

“This too must be gold by morning,” he said.

Again she wept.

Again the little man appeared.

This time she gave him her ring.

And again…

The straw became gold.

On the third night, the King led her into the largest chamber of all.

“If you succeed this time,” he said, “you shall be my queen.”

But when the little man came again, she had nothing left to give.

He leaned close and whispered,

“Then promise me your first child, when you are queen.”

Her heart trembled.

But she saw no other way.

And she agreed.


Soon, she became Queen.

And in time, she had a beautiful baby.

For a while, she forgot her promise.

But one quiet evening…

The little man returned.

“You promised,” he said softly.

The Queen fell to her knees.

She offered gold. She offered jewels.

But he shook his head.

At last, he said,

“I will give you three days. If you can tell me my name… You may keep your child.”

The Queen searched everywhere.

She sent messengers across the land.

She stayed awake through long nights, whispering every name she could remember.

The first day, she guessed many names.

But each time, the little man said,

“That is not my name.”

The second day, she guessed strange and silly names.

But still…

“That is not my name.”


On the third day, a messenger returned from the forest.

He said,

“I saw a strange little man dancing by a fire, singing,

‘Tomorrow I bake, today I brew, Little does my lady know… Rumpelstiltskin is my name.’”

The Queen’s heart leapt with joy.

That night, the little man came again.

He smiled proudly.

“Now, what is my name?”

She guessed once.

“No.”

She guessed again.

“No.”

Then she looked at him gently and said,

“Is your name… Rumpelstiltskin?”


The little man cried out in anger.

He stamped his foot.

He shouted in rage.

And then…

He vanished forever.

The Queen held her child close.

And never again was she afraid.


And so the girl who once wept in a locked room…

Became a wise Queen.

And the strange little man…

Was never seen again.

THE END!
