The Wonderful Wizard of Oz.


Once, in a quiet land of wide gray fields, there lived a little girl named Dorothy.

She lived with Aunt Em and Uncle Henry, and her very best friend in the world was a small black dog named Toto.
Though the prairie was dull and the days were simple, Dorothy’s heart was bright.

One afternoon, when the sky darkened and the wind began to sing in a low, uneasy voice, something extraordinary happened.

A great cyclone lifted Dorothy’s little house gently into the air…
and carried it far, far away.

When the house finally came to rest, Dorothy opened the door and found herself in a land of color and music.

She had arrived in Oz.

Little people called Munchkins gathered around her, amazed and grateful, for her house had accidentally fallen upon the Wicked Witch of the East, freeing them from fear.

{{1.png}}

Soon, a kind and gentle sorceress appeared.

She was Glinda, the Good Witch of the North.

Glinda kissed Dorothy’s forehead for protection and placed upon her feet the silver shoes that had once belonged to the Wicked Witch.

“You must follow the Yellow Brick Road,” Glinda said softly.
“It will lead you to the Emerald City, where the great Wizard of Oz may help you return home.”

And so Dorothy began her journey, with Toto trotting faithfully beside her.

Not long after she started, Dorothy saw a lonely figure standing in a cornfield.

It was a Scarecrow, stuffed with straw and tied to a pole.

To her surprise, he could speak.

He told her he wished for one thing more than anything else…
brains, so he would no longer feel foolish.

Dorothy smiled kindly and invited him to join her on the road.

{{2.png}}

And together they walked on.

Soon they heard a sad, hollow sound in the forest.

There they found a man made entirely of tin.

He stood frozen in place, rusted stiff.

With gentle care, Dorothy and the Scarecrow oiled his joints and set him free.

He was the Tin Woodman, and his wish was simple.

He wanted a heart, so he could feel love again.

He, too, joined the journey.

Not long after, a mighty Lion leapt onto the path.

He roared terribly… but when Dorothy bravely scolded him for frightening Toto, the great beast lowered his head in shame.

“I am a coward,” he confessed.
“I want nothing more than courage.”

{{3.png}}

Dorothy held out her hand, and the Lion walked beside them from that moment on.

So now there were four friends on the Yellow Brick Road.

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Cowardly Lion.

Each with a wish.
Each with hope.

They crossed deep forests and wide rivers.

They outwitted fierce Kalidahs.
They built rafts and crossed rushing waters.
They walked through fields of beautiful but deadly poppies, where Dorothy and the Lion fell asleep until tiny field mice came to the rescue.

At last, shining in the distance, they saw it.

The Emerald City.

In the great green city, they were welcomed with wonder.

{{4.png}}

But the Wizard of Oz, powerful and mysterious, gave them a task before granting their wishes.

“You must defeat the Wicked Witch of the West,” he commanded.

Though afraid, the friends set out once more.

The Wicked Witch watched them closely.

She sent wolves, crows, and bees, but each time the friends helped one another and stayed safe.

Finally, the Witch used her most powerful magic.

She sent the Winged Monkeys.

Dorothy and her friends were captured and taken to the Witch’s dark castle.

The Scarecrow was torn apart.
The Tin Woodman was bent and broken.
The Lion was chained.
And Dorothy was forced to work.

{{5.png}}

But even in that gloomy place, Dorothy kept her courage.

One day, when the Witch tried to steal Dorothy’s silver shoes, the girl acted without thinking.

She threw a bucket of water.

And with a quiet cry, the Wicked Witch melted away.

The Winkies, who had long lived in fear, were finally free.

They repaired the Tin Woodman, stuffed the Scarecrow once more, and thanked Dorothy with gentle hearts.

Soon, the friends returned to the Emerald City.

The Wizard greeted them at last.

He gave the Scarecrow a diploma, teaching him that he had been wise all along.

He gave the Tin Woodman a ticking heart, showing him he had always been kind.

{{6.png}}

He gave the Lion a medal, and with it came true courage.

But when Dorothy asked to go home… the Wizard’s balloon lifted without her.

She was left behind.

Dorothy felt her hope fade… until Glinda appeared once more.

“My dear,” the Good Witch said softly, “you had the power to go home all along. The silver shoes will take you there.”

With tears in her eyes, Dorothy hugged her friends.

She tapped her heels together and whispered her wish.

“There is no place like home.”

The wind lifted her gently.

And when she opened her eyes…

She was back in Kansas.

Aunt Em held her close.
Uncle Henry smiled.
And Toto barked with joy.

{{7.png}}

And so, the girl who traveled beyond the rainbow learned something precious.

That courage can live in the shyest heart.
That kindness can beat in the quietest chest.
That wisdom can grow even in a head of straw.

And that home…

is the place where love is waiting.
