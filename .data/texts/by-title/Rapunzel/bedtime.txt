Rapunzel

(A calm bedtime retelling)

Long ago, in a quiet village near a high stone wall, there lived a man and his wife who wished with all their hearts for a child.

For many years, their wish did not come true.

One spring morning, as the woman stood by a small window at the back of their house, she looked into a beautiful garden beyond the wall. It belonged to a powerful enchantress, and in it grew the freshest, greenest rampion.

The woman longed for it so deeply that her strength began to fade.

Her husband, seeing her sorrow, said gently, “I will bring you what you need, no matter the cost.”

That evening, he climbed over the wall and picked a handful of rampion. His wife ate it gladly. But the next day she longed for more, and more the day after that.

So once again, the man climbed into the garden.

This time, the enchantress stood waiting.

“How dare you steal from my garden?” she said.

The man fell to his knees and told her everything. “My wife is ill with longing,” he said. “She would die without it.”

The enchantress listened in silence, then spoke. “You may take as much rampion as you wish. But in return, you must give me the child your wife will soon bear. I will care for the child as my own.”

In fear, the man agreed.

When the baby girl was born, the enchantress came and took her away. She named her Rapunzel.

Rapunzel grew into the most beautiful child in the world. Her hair shone like spun gold, and her voice was gentle and sweet.

When she was twelve years old, the enchantress placed her in a tall tower deep in the forest. The tower had no door and no stairs, only a small window at the top.

Whenever the enchantress wished to enter, she would stand below and call,
“Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair to me.”

Rapunzel would unfasten her long braids, and they would fall like golden ribbons. The enchantress would climb up.

For many years, Rapunzel lived quietly in the tower. She sang to the birds, spoke to the wind, and dreamed of the world she had never seen.

One day, someone heard her song.

A young prince rode through the forest and stopped when her voice floated through the trees. He searched for a door but found none. Each day he returned, standing beneath the tower, listening.

One evening, he saw the enchantress arrive and heard her call. He watched Rapunzel’s hair fall and understood.

The next night, the prince stood below and called,
“Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair to me.”

Rapunzel let her hair fall, and the prince climbed up.

At first, she was frightened. She had never seen anyone but the enchantress. But the prince spoke kindly and told her how her song had touched his heart. Slowly, fear faded into trust.

They spoke often after that. Each evening he came, and Rapunzel’s lonely days grew brighter.

One night, the prince asked Rapunzel to go away with him and be his wife. She said yes, but she did not know how to leave the tower.

So they made a plan. Each time he came, he would bring a strand of silk. Rapunzel would weave a ladder, and when it was ready, she would climb down.

But one day, without thinking, Rapunzel said to the enchantress, “Why is it that you are so much heavier to pull up than the young prince?”

The enchantress froze. Then her anger burst like a storm.

In her rage, she cut off Rapunzel’s beautiful hair and sent her far away into a lonely desert, where Rapunzel lived in grief and quiet strength.

That same day, the enchantress tied the cut hair to the tower window.

When the prince came that evening and climbed up, he found the enchantress waiting. She laughed cruelly and told him Rapunzel was gone forever.

In despair, the prince leapt from the tower. He did not die, but the thorns below pierced his eyes, and he became blind.

For many years, he wandered the forest in sorrow, living on roots and berries, never stopping his search or his love.

Far away, Rapunzel had given birth to twins, a boy and a girl. She cared for them with gentle strength.

One day, the wandering prince heard a familiar voice singing. He followed it.

When Rapunzel saw him, she ran to him and wept. Two of her tears fell upon his eyes, and his sight was restored.

They held each other for a long time, and the years of sorrow slowly melted away.

The prince led Rapunzel and the children back to his kingdom, where they were welcomed with joy.

And from that day on, they lived in peace and happiness.

The girl once locked in a tower found her way into the world.
The prince who once walked in darkness found his light again.
And love, gentle and patient, won in the end.
