The Swineherd

(A calm bedtime retelling)


Once there was a Prince who lived in a country of green fields and clear skies.

He was kind.

And he wished to marry someone kind, too.

So he thought of a Princess in a faraway palace.

People said she was very grand.

Very proud.

But the Prince hoped she also had a gentle heart.


He sent her two gifts.

The first was a rose.

Not one from a garden full of roses…

But a rose that had grown in his own yard.

The only rose.

So precious that he had watched it open day by day.


The second gift was a nightingale.

A little bird that could sing so sweetly it sounded like moonlight.


The gifts arrived at the Princess’s palace.

The Princess looked at the rose.

She sniffed.

“It is only a real rose,” she said.


She listened to the nightingale.

She frowned.

“It is only a real bird,” she said.


And she sent the gifts away.


When the Prince heard this, he felt a quiet sadness.

So he decided to see the Princess for himself.

But he would not go as a Prince.

Not with trumpets.

Not with bright coats.


He put on simple clothes.

He traveled alone.

And when he reached the palace gates, he asked for work.


The palace guards laughed.

But they needed someone to help in the yard.

So they gave him a small place to sleep.

And a job caring for the pigs.


So the Prince became…

A swineherd.


In the evenings, when the palace grew quiet, the swineherd made little things.

Clever things.

Small and shining.


First he made a tiny pot.

When it boiled, it did something surprising.

It could tell you what was cooking in every kitchen in the city.


The Princess heard of it.

She peered through the window.

“I want that pot,” she said.


So she walked down into the yard.

“How much for the pot?” she asked.


“Ten kisses,” said the swineherd.


The Princess blinked.

“Ten?”


“Yes,” said the swineherd.


The Princess did not like the idea.

But she liked the pot.

So she called her ladies to stand around her like a wall.

And she gave ten quick kisses.


The swineherd handed over the pot.


Another evening, he made a little rattle.

When you turned it, it played every song you could think of.

Marches.

Lullabies.

Dances.


The Princess heard.

She came again.

“How much for the rattle?”


“One hundred kisses,” said the swineherd.


The Princess gasped.

But the rattle made music like a dream.

And she wanted it.


So again, she asked her ladies to hide her.

And again… she paid.


But this time the Emperor—her father—walked past.

He saw his daughter in the yard.

He saw the swineherd.

He heard the counting.


He was shocked.

He was angry.


“Out!” he shouted.

“Out of my palace, both of you!”


The Princess began to cry.

The swineherd bowed.

And they were sent away.


Outside the palace walls, the wind blew cool.

The Princess looked down at her fine shoes.

At her soft cloak.

At the dark road.


“I have lost everything,” she whispered.


The swineherd stopped.

He looked at her kindly… and sadly.


“Do you know who I am?” he asked.


And then he took off his simple cap.

And stood taller.

And the Princess saw…

A Prince.


“I came with a rose,” he said.

“And a nightingale.

You did not want them.

You wanted tricks instead of true gifts.

So I learned something, too.”


The Princess’s cheeks warmed.

She remembered the rose.

She remembered the bird’s song.

And for the first time… she wished she had listened.


The Prince spoke gently.

“A kind heart is worth more than any clever toy.

I cannot marry someone who does not see that.”


And the Prince walked on.

Toward home.

Toward a life where real roses mattered.


The Princess stood very still.

And though the night was quiet…

It felt like a lesson had finally begun.


The End!
