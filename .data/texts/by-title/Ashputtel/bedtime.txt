Ashputtel

(A calm bedtime retelling)


Long ago, in a quiet countryside, there lived a kind little girl whose life began with love… and soon met with sorrow.

Her mother fell very ill.

And before she closed her eyes forever, she called her daughter close and whispered, “Always be good, my child. I will watch over you from heaven.”

Not long after, her mother was laid to rest in the garden.

And every day, the little girl went there to weep… and to remember.

When spring came, her father married again.

His new wife brought with her two daughters of her own.

They were pretty in face… but unkind in heart.

They took away the little girl’s fine clothes. They gave her an old gray dress. They sent her to sleep by the hearth among the ashes.

And so they called her Ashputtel.

From morning until night, Ashputtel worked.

She carried water. She made the fire. She cooked and cleaned.

And when she was tired…

She lay by the warm ashes and closed her eyes.

But she never forgot her mother.

One day, her father was going to the fair.

He asked his stepdaughters what gifts they wanted.

“Fine clothes,” said one. “Jewels,” said the other.

Then he turned to Ashputtel.

“My child, what would you like?”

She answered softly, “Bring me the first twig that brushes your hat on your way home.”

When her father returned, he gave her the twig.

Ashputtel planted it beside her mother’s grave.

She watered it with her tears.

And from that twig grew a beautiful hazel tree.

Soon a little white bird came and made its home there.

And from that day on, the bird watched over Ashputtel.



Not long after, the King announced a grand feast that would last three days.

At this feast, his son would choose a bride.

Ashputtel’s stepsisters were invited.

They made her brush their shoes. Comb their hair. Tie their ribbons.

Ashputtel did as she was told… but her heart felt heavy.

She longed to go too.

She begged her stepmother.

But the woman laughed.

“If you can pick this dish of peas from the ashes in two hours, you may go.”

She threw the peas into the hearth.

Ashputtel ran into the garden and cried softly,

“Little birds of the sky, come help me.”

White doves flew in. Turtle-doves followed. And then all the small birds of the air.

Together they picked every grain from the ashes.

When Ashputtel returned with the full dish, her stepmother only gave her more work.

Again the birds helped her.

Again the woman refused her.

At last, when everyone had gone to the feast, Ashputtel stood beneath the hazel tree and whispered,

“Shake, shake, hazel tree, Gold and silver over me.”


The white bird flew down.

And suddenly Ashputtel stood in a gown of silver and gold… with shining slippers on her feet.

She hurried to the feast.

No one recognized her.

Not her sisters. Not her father.

But the Prince saw her.

And from the first dance… he danced with no one else.

All evening he stayed by her side.

When night came, Ashputtel slipped away.

She ran through the garden… changed back into her gray dress… and lay again by the ashes.

The next night, the same thing happened.

And the night after that.

Each time, her gown was finer. Each time, the Prince loved her more.

On the third night, as Ashputtel hurried away, one of her golden slippers slipped from her foot.

The Prince found it.

And he made a promise.

“I will marry the girl whose foot fits this shoe.”


The next day, he went from house to house.

Ashputtel’s stepsisters tried the slipper.

One cut her toe. One cut her heel.

But the slipper did not fit.

And the white dove in the hazel tree sang,

“This is not the true bride.”

At last, Ashputtel was called.

She washed her face. She stepped forward.

She placed her foot in the slipper.

And it fit perfectly.

The Prince looked at her face.

And he knew.

“This is the one.”


Ashputtel rode away with the Prince.

Not as a servant. Not as a girl in ashes.

But as a bride.

And from that day on, she lived in kindness and peace.

And the little white bird stayed with her… as her mother once promised.

Watching.

Loving.

Always near.

The End!
